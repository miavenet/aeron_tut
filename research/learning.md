# Learning-method evidence and limits

- Retrieval practice: Henry L. Roediger III and Jeffrey D. Karpicke (2006), “Test-Enhanced Learning: Taking Memory Tests Improves Long-Term Retention,” *Psychological Science* 17(3), [DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x/). Their experiments distinguish immediate performance from later retention; they do not establish an effect size for learning Aeron.
- Distributed practice: Nicholas J. Cepeda, Harold Pashler, Edward Vul, John T. Wixted, and Doug Rohrer (2006), “Distributed Practice in Verbal Recall Tasks: A Review and Quantitative Synthesis,” *Psychological Bulletin* 132(3), [DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354). Spacing effects depend on the retention interval and task. The course's +1/+3/+7/+14 policy is an engineering choice, not a universally optimal prescription.
- Recent syntheses: Shana K. Carpenter, Steven C. Pan, and Andrew C. Butler (2022), “The science of effective learning with spacing and retrieval practice,” *Nature Reviews Psychology* 1(9), 496–511, [DOI 10.1038/s44159-022-00089-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44159-022-00089-1); and Pooja K. Agarwal, Ludmila D. Nunes, and Janell R. Blunt (2021), “Retrieval Practice Consistently Benefits Student Learning: a Systematic Review of Applied Research in Schools and Classrooms,” *Educational Psychology Review* 33(4), 1409–1453, [DOI 10.1007/s10648-021-09595-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09595-9). They review studies of other learners and materials; they do not validate this course's intervals or time estimates.

This course has not measured novice completion time or one-month retention. Its learning and timing targets remain design hypotheses.
